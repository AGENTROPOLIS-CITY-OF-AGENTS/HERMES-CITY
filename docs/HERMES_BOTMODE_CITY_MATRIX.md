# HERMES Bot Mode City Matrix

HERMES CITY exposes a public-safe view of the Bot Mode organization model.

## Canonical operating principle

HERMES is not organized as a flat swarm and not as a rigid bureaucracy.

It is a governed matrix city:

- Verticals own outcomes.
- Horizontals own standards.
- Mission Pods do the work.
- Layer-1 governance owns authority.
- Human Mission Control retains sovereignty.

## Vertical integration

Verticals are end-to-end district, product, civic-service, or platform chains. They carry mission accountability from research and data through engineering, operations, distribution, and support.

Examples include HERMES Runtime, HERMES CITY, Creator, Entertainment, Gaming, Sports Grid, Fashion, Commerce, GTM, Docking, Infrastructure Utilities, and Civic Administration.

## Horizontal integration

Horizontal functions provide expertise and standards across many verticals:

- Research and Development
- Engineering
- Product
- Design
- Security
- 54-T Governance
- Evaluation and Assurance
- Finance
- Legal and Licensing
- Data and Provenance
- Infrastructure and SRE
- GTM and Distribution
- People and Organization
- Procurement and Vendor Risk
- Communications

Horizontal functions do not automatically receive unrestricted cross-city authority. Permissions remain mission-scoped and policy-gated.

## Research and Development

R&D is a first-class institution and remains distinct from routine production engineering.

Research lanes include fundamental and applied AI, agent systems, model architecture, post-training, inference, quantization, memory, retrieval, robotics, simulation, spatial computing, HCI, security, cryptography, evaluation science, BE, assurance, economics, interoperability, and civic systems.

Public research lifecycle:

```text
hypothesis
-> sandbox
-> experiment
-> benchmark
-> adversarial review
-> reproducibility
-> assurance
-> pilot
-> production candidate
```

An experiment does not grant itself production authority.

## Mission Pods

Mission Pods are temporary, cross-functional teams assembled by HERMES Dispatch for a specific objective.

Every pod should resolve, at minimum:

- mission
- vertical owner
- pod lead
- required horizontal functions
- approved models
- approved skills
- approved tools
- data scope
- memory scope
- compute budget
- risk tier
- approval gates
- receipt schema
- escalation path

Lifecycle:

```text
classify
-> assign vertical owner
-> resolve horizontal functions
-> select minimum capability surface
-> gate
-> instantiate
-> execute
-> verify
-> receipt
-> archive
-> hibernate or dissolve
```

This follows the Quantization Torque doctrine: use the smallest verified capability and context surface that can safely complete the mission.

## Public/private boundary

HERMES CITY may expose organizational taxonomy, non-sensitive role topology, department relationships, mission contract shapes, and research lifecycles.

Private Agentropolis systems retain production prompts, credentials, secret values, internal execution graphs, private model/tool policies, sensitive memory, citizen or client data, and sovereign runtime controls.
